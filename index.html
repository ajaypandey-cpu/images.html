Ernesto "Che" Guevara was an Argentine-born Marxist revolutionary, physician, and author who became a central figure in the Cuban Revolution. Born into a middle-class family in 1928, his perspective shifted dramatically during a motorcycle journey across South America, where he witnessed extreme poverty and social injustice. This experience convinced him that armed revolution was the only way to eliminate capitalism and imperialism in Latin America. In 1955, Guevara met Fidel Castro in Mexico and joined the 26th of July Movement. He proved to be a brilliant and disciplined military tactician, playing a decisive role in overthrowing Cuban dictator Fulgencio Batista in 1959. Following the revolution, he served in high-ranking government positions, managing land reforms and acting as the president of Cuba’s national bank. Driven by a desire to spark global socialist uprisings, he later left Cuba to train guerrilla forces in the Congo and Bolivia. In 1967, he was captured by CIA-assisted Bolivian forces and executed at the age of 39.Today, Guevara remains a highly polarized figure. Critics condemn him as a ruthless, authoritarian militant who sanctioned executions without trial. Conversely, supporters revere him as a heroic symbol of anti-imperialism, self-sacrifice, and idealistic rebellion. His stylized portrait, captured in Alberto Korda's famous photograph Guerrillero Heroico, endures worldwide as an omnipresent cultural icon of resistance.
